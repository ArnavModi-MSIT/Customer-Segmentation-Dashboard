🎯 Project Overview

This project analyzes customer purchasing behavior using Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) analysis — a rule-based scoring technique, not machine learning. Customers are segmented into seven groups to support targeted retention and engagement strategies. All metrics are computed on the true customer identity (customer_unique_id), not the per-order key the raw dataset exposes by default — a distinction that materially changes every downstream number.

Customer Segments

  • Champions: Best customers, highest value
  • Loyal Customers: Consistent purchasers
  • Potential Loyalist: High conversion potential
  • Need Attention: Largest segment by size
  • At Risk: Elevated recency, previously high value
  • New Customers: Recent, low frequency
  • Lost: Inactive for an extended period

🛠 Tech Stack

A Python/pandas pipeline that reads raw CSVs and writes a single Excel workbook — no database, no credentials — with all business metrics served through Power BI.

Business Intelligence

Key Business Insights

📈

Revenue Concentration

Champions (7% of customers) generate 13.15% of revenue — a real but modest concentration, not the inflated 80/20-style split a naive analysis can produce when customer identity is computed incorrectly.

🎯

Segmentation Reality Check

"Need Attention" is the largest segment at ~40% of customers — a direct consequence of a 3.05% repeat-purchase rate in this dataset. Most customers are one-time buyers, which shapes every retention strategy built on top of this data.

💰

Revenue at Risk

R$7.0M — 45.6% of total revenue — sits with customers in the At Risk, Need Attention, or Lost segments. No prediction model involved: this is their own historical spend, grouped by how recently and how often they've bought.

📊

Data-Driven Segmentation

RFM scoring on the correct customer identity provides a quantifiable, reproducible basis for customer valuation, replacing per-order artifacts with genuine customer-level metrics — recomputed from a plain Excel workbook, no database required.

🛒

Product & Category Mix

Health & beauty, watches & gifts, and bed/bath/table lead category revenue — the product-performance page breaks down exactly which categories are carrying the business.

💵

Retention ROI

Recovering just 20% of the "At Risk" segment's historical spend would add roughly R$641K in revenue — modeled directly in the dashboard's retention scenarios, transparently and without an ML probability score.

Technical Approach

Methodology & Implementation

01

Prepare Data

Raw Olist CSVs read directly with pandas — no database — with product category nulls cleaned and English category names joined in.

02

EDA

Revenue, order, customer, and review metrics computed on order-level (not item-level) aggregates for correctness.

03

RFM Segmentation

Recency, Frequency, and Monetary scores computed on true customer identity — rule-based quantile scoring, mapping customers to 7 segments.

04

Business Insights

Revenue concentration, at-risk revenue, and retention ROI scenarios computed directly from the RFM segments — every number traceable to a sum or a percentage.

05

Excel Export

Every table written to one multi-sheet Excel workbook — the single file that stands in for a data warehouse in this project.

06

Visualization

The Excel workbook is read directly by Power BI, with relationships and DAX measures built on top, served through an interactive dashboard across 4 pages.
